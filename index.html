About

Biography

Central University of Kerala, Kasaragod

Ravi Kumar is a Senior Research Fellow and PhD candidate in the Department of International Relations and Political Science at the Central University of Kerala. His doctoral thesis, Status, Regions, and Rising Powers in the Emerging World Order: A Case Study of India, has been submitted, and the viva is awaited.

His research examines status and recognition in international relations, region formation and rising-power strategy, India’s foreign and nuclear policy, and the Indo-Pacific. The thesis develops Regional Status Theory, which holds that rising powers build and lead regions to secure recognition, and tests it across four Indian cases from 1945 to the present using archival sources, a coded corpus of parliamentary debate and Ministry of External Affairs records. His articles are published in or forthcoming from India Review, World Affairs and other journals, and he has released open datasets of Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha debates on Harvard Dataverse.

He holds an MPhil in Political Science from the University of Rajasthan, and an MA in Political Science (University Gold Medal) and an MSc in Mathematics from Maharshi Dayanand University. He is UGC-NET qualified in Political Science and in International and Area Studies.

Research

Research Profile

My research asks how rising powers seek recognition of higher rank in international politics. My doctoral thesis develops Regional Status Theory: rising powers build and lead regions to secure recognition, rather than acquiring regional leadership as a by-product of rising. I test the theory across four Indian cases from 1945 to the present, using archival sources, a coded corpus of parliamentary debate and Ministry of External Affairs records.

A second line of work studies how India’s foreign and nuclear policy has been argued in Parliament, using open corpora of Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha debates from 1952 to the present. A third examines status competition and maritime strategy in the Indo-Pacific, including India’s SAGAR initiative.

Notes on Finished Books

Short notes on 37 titles.

Constructivism IR Theory

Social Theory of International Politics

Alexander Wendt

Wendt argues that anarchy is not a fixed fact of world politics. It is something states make through the way they treat each other. The book is the main statement of constructivism, and it shows how shared ideas shape what states want and who they think they are.

Status in World Order Status-Seekers

Quest for Status: Chinese and Russian Foreign Policy

Deborah Welch Larson & Alexei Shevchenko

A study of how Russia and China have gone after recognition as great powers since the end of the Cold War. The authors compare the different routes each has taken, from trying to join the club to competing with it.

Status in World Order

The Making of International Status

Marina Duque

Duque treats status as something built in the interactions between states, not something a state simply owns. I keep it close as an anchor for the status literature I work with.

Regional History & Regional Security South Asia

The Most Dangerous Place: A History of the United States in South Asia

Srinath Raghavan

A history of how the United States has shaped South Asia since the 1940s, and how the region pushed back on American policy. Raghavan follows the ties with India, Pakistan and Afghanistan through the Cold War and after.

Regional History & Regional Security South Asia

Pakistan’s Nuclear Bomb: A Story of Defiance, Deterrence and Deviance

Hassan Abbas

Abbas traces how Pakistan got the bomb and why it wanted it. The story runs through the army, the scientists and the procurement networks that kept the programme going.

Regional History & Regional Security South Asia

Pakistan Between Mosque and Military

Husain Haqqani

Haqqani looks at how Pakistan’s army and Islamist groups came to work together, and what that tie did to the country’s search for an identity and for security.

Indian Military History & Military Leadership

General Brasstacks: The Sundarji Story

Probal Dasgupta

A biography of General Krishnaswamy Sundarji, the Army chief behind Operation Brasstacks. It shows his part in reshaping the Indian Army in the 1980s.

Intelligence Indian Politics

The Khalistan Conspiracy

G. B. S. Sidhu

A former R&AW officer’s account of the Khalistan movement in the 1980s. It covers the intelligence side of the story and the politics that surrounded it.

Intelligence Geopolitics

Great Power Games

Vikram Sood

A former R&AW chief’s account of a world order in flux, from the end of the Second World War to the present. Sood frames it as a shift from Western decline to Eastern ascent and reads India’s place in it through intelligence and statecraft.

Military History India-Pakistan

Operation Sindoor: The Untold Story of India’s Deep Strikes Inside Pakistan

Lt Gen. K. J. S. ‘Tiny’ Dhillon (Retd)

A retired general’s account of Operation Sindoor, India’s strikes inside Pakistan in 2025, and the military thinking behind them.

Military History India-China

1962: The War That Wasn’t

Shiv Kunal Verma

A detailed military history of the 1962 war with China, built on field visits, regimental records and interviews with officers who were there. Verma goes back over how the war is usually told.

World Order IR Theory

World Order

Henry Kissinger

Kissinger walks through how Europe, the Islamic world, China and the United States each built their own idea of order, and why that patchwork is straining now. I read it alongside Acharya and the multiplex literature.

A narrative history of the last years of British India, told through Mountbatten, Nehru, Jinnah, Gandhi and Edwina. It reads quickly and is good on how personal chemistry shaped the end of the empire.

Marshall’s follow-up looks at more places where geography still sets the limits, from Australia and the Sahel to space. It is a quick way to see how popular geopolitics describes the regions I work on.
