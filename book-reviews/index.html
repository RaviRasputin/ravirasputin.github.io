World Order IR Theory

The Once and Future World Order: Why Global Civilization Will Survive the Decline of the West

Amitav Acharya

Acharya argues that global civilisation will outlast the decline of Western primacy, extending his earlier work on multiplexity. The review takes the book seriously as theory and tests its claims against rival accounts of the present transition.

Commonwealth & Comparative Politics · Mar 2026 · DOI 10.1080/14662043.2026.2631118 Published
2026
Diplomatic History Indian Foreign Policy

Nehru’s First Recruits: The Diplomats Who Built Independent India’s Foreign Policy

Kallol Bhattacherjee

A group portrait of the early diplomats who staffed India’s foreign service and shaped its initial conduct on the world stage. The review treats the book as biographical history with implications for how Indian diplomatic identity was made.

Journal of Asian Security and International Affairs · Feb 2026 · DOI 10.1177/23477970251401280 Published
2025
World Order Indian Foreign Policy

The New World: 21st Century Global Order and India

Ram Madhav

Madhav offers a reading of the emerging twenty-first century order and India’s role within it. The review locates the book in the wider debate on civilisational and ideological framings of Indian foreign policy.

India’s World Magazine · November 2025 Published
2025
World Order IR Theory

Divergent Worlds: What the Ancient Mediterranean and Indian Ocean Can Tell Us about the Future of International Order

Amitav Acharya & Manjeet S. Pardesi

A comparative inquiry into the ancient Mediterranean and Indian Ocean systems and what they suggest about the futures available to the present order. The review reads the book as an extension of Acharya’s multiplex framework and a sustained provocation to Eurocentric IR.

India Quarterly · May 2025 Accepted

Diplomacy Indian Foreign Policy

Negotiating India’s Landmark Agreements

A. S. Bhasin

Bhasin reconstructs the bargaining behind several of independent India’s most consequential agreements, drawing on archival material and personal access. The review treats the book as a contribution to negotiation history rather than to IR theory.

Strategic Analysis · May 2025 · DOI 10.1080/09700161.2025.2494944 Published
2025
Indian Foreign Policy World Order

Vishwa Shastra: India and the World

Dhruva Jaishankar

A wide-ranging survey of India’s evolving worldview and its place in the present international system. The review weighs the book’s synthetic ambition against the question of whether it adds an analytical frame to the historical record.

The Round Table · Apr 2025 · DOI 10.1080/00358533.2025.2478119 Published
2025
India-China Diplomatic History

Crosswinds: Nehru, Zhou and the Anglo-American Competition over China

Vijay Gokhale

Gokhale revisits the Nehru-Zhou years through the wider Anglo-American contest for influence over Beijing. The review reads the book both as diplomatic history and as a former practitioner’s reading of Indian China policy.

China Information · Feb 2025 · DOI 10.1177/0920203X251318173a Published
2024
Status Politics Rising Powers

The Unfinished Quest: India’s Search for Major Power Status from Nehru to Modi

T. V. Paul

Paul argues that India has long pursued major power status without converting recognition into structural standing. A central reference for contemporary debates on Indian status politics and a recurring point of engagement in my own work.

International Relations of the Asia-Pacific · Sep 2024 · DOI 10.1093/irap/lcae014 Published

India-China World Order

The Sino-Indian Rivalry: Implications for Global Order

Sumit Ganguly, Manjeet S. Pardesi & William R. Thompson

The authors trace the long arc of Sino-Indian competition and ask whether it carries weight for the wider order. The review situates the book within strategic rivalry theory and against alternative readings of India-China competition.

International Journal · May 2024 · DOI 10.1177/00207020241257637 Published
