Indian Foreign Policy Multipolarity

India After Nonalignment: The Quest to Become a Pole in International Politics

Happymon Jacob

Jacob traces India’s move beyond non-alignment and its quest to become a pole in international politics. Directly relevant to my work on India’s status seeking and the multiplex world order.

Notes on Finished Books

Short notes on 37 titles.

Constructivism IR Theory

Social Theory of International Politics

Alexander Wendt

Wendt argues that anarchy is not a fixed fact of world politics. It is something states make through the way they treat each other. The book is the main statement of constructivism, and it shows how shared ideas shape what states want and who they think they are.

Status in World Order Status-Seekers

Quest for Status: Chinese and Russian Foreign Policy

Deborah Welch Larson & Alexei Shevchenko

A study of how Russia and China have gone after recognition as great powers since the end of the Cold War. The authors compare the different routes each has taken, from trying to join the club to competing with it.

Status in World Order

The Making of International Status

Marina Duque

Duque treats status as something built in the interactions between states, not something a state simply owns. I keep it close as an anchor for the status literature I work with.

Regional History & Regional Security South Asia

The Most Dangerous Place: A History of the United States in South Asia

Srinath Raghavan

A history of how the United States has shaped South Asia since the 1940s, and how the region pushed back on American policy. Raghavan follows the ties with India, Pakistan and Afghanistan through the Cold War and after.

Regional History & Regional Security South Asia

Pakistan’s Nuclear Bomb: A Story of Defiance, Deterrence and Deviance

Hassan Abbas

Abbas traces how Pakistan got the bomb and why it wanted it. The story runs through the army, the scientists and the procurement networks that kept the programme going.

Regional History & Regional Security South Asia

Pakistan Between Mosque and Military

Husain Haqqani

Haqqani looks at how Pakistan’s army and Islamist groups came to work together, and what that tie did to the country’s search for an identity and for security.

Indian Military History & Military Leadership

General Brasstacks: The Sundarji Story

Probal Dasgupta

A biography of General Krishnaswamy Sundarji, the Army chief behind Operation Brasstacks. It shows his part in reshaping the Indian Army in the 1980s.

Intelligence Indian Politics

The Khalistan Conspiracy

G. B. S. Sidhu

A former R&AW officer’s account of the Khalistan movement in the 1980s. It covers the intelligence side of the story and the politics that surrounded it.

Intelligence Geopolitics

Great Power Games

Vikram Sood

A former R&AW chief’s account of a world order in flux, from the end of the Second World War to the present. Sood frames it as a shift from Western decline to Eastern ascent and reads India’s place in it through intelligence and statecraft.

Military History India-Pakistan

Operation Sindoor: The Untold Story of India’s Deep Strikes Inside Pakistan

Lt Gen. K. J. S. ‘Tiny’ Dhillon (Retd)

A retired general’s account of Operation Sindoor, India’s strikes inside Pakistan in 2025, and the military thinking behind them.

Military History India-China

1962: The War That Wasn’t

Shiv Kunal Verma

A detailed military history of the 1962 war with China, built on field visits, regimental records and interviews with officers who were there. Verma goes back over how the war is usually told.

World Order IR Theory

World Order

Henry Kissinger

Kissinger walks through how Europe, the Islamic world, China and the United States each built their own idea of order, and why that patchwork is straining now. I read it alongside Acharya and the multiplex literature.

Partition Indian History

Indian Summer: The Secret History of the End of an Empire

Alex von Tunzelmann

A narrative history of the last years of British India, told through Mountbatten, Nehru, Jinnah, Gandhi and Edwina. It reads quickly and is good on how personal chemistry shaped the end of the empire.

World Order Indian Foreign Policy

The New World: 21st Century Global Order and India

Ram Madhav

Madhav’s view of the changing global order and where India sits in it. I reviewed it for India’s World Magazine in November 2025.

Geopolitics Strategy

The Power of Geography

Tim Marshall

Marshall’s follow-up looks at more places where geography still sets the limits, from Australia and the Sahel to space. It is a quick way to see how popular geopolitics describes the regions I work on.

Geopolitics Strategy

Prisoners of Geography

Tim Marshall

Marshall explains how rivers, mountains and coastlines keep shaping what states can do, one region at a time. Easy to read, and a good first look at the map.

India-China Indian Foreign Policy

Self-Deception: India’s China Policies

Arun Shourie

Shourie’s sharp critique of India’s China policy, arguing that the same mistaken assumptions kept coming back. I checked it against the archival accounts to see where the record disagrees.

Northeast India History

His Majesty’s Headhunters: The Siege of Kohima that Shaped World History

Mmhonlümo Kikon

An account of the 1944 Siege of Kohima with the Naga people at the centre. Kikon argues that the battle, sometimes called the Stalingrad of the East, mattered far beyond the hills of Nagaland.

India-China Strategy

Rising to the China Challenge: Winning Through Strategic Patience and Economic Growth

Gautam Bambawale

Bambawale, a former ambassador to China, argues that after Doklam and Ladakh India should expect a long and hostile relationship. His answer is strategic patience and economic growth.

Indian History Princely States

Dethroned: Patel, Menon and the Integration of Princely India

John Zubrzycki

How the Indian princes went from Britain’s most loyal allies to the losers of independence. Zubrzycki tells it as a story of promises, pressure and intrigue as the states were drawn into the Union.

Indian Politics Ambedkar

Worshipping False Gods

Arun Shourie

A hard-hitting critique of Ambedkar and his place in Indian politics, first published in 1997 and still argued over. I read it for the debate around it as much as for its claims.

IR Theory Realism

The Tragedy of Great Power Politics

John J. Mearsheimer

The standard statement of offensive realism: great powers fear each other, so they try to be as powerful as they can. I go back to it whenever I need the material-power view of rising powers.

Indian History Nation Building

1947-1957, India: The Birth of a Republic

Chandrachur Ghose

A look at the first ten years of independent India, from 1947 to 1957, drawing on archival material. It covers the choices that set the course of the republic and is useful background for the Nehru years.

The Policy PivotEdited by Ajay Khanna and Rahul Sharma
Indian Foreign Policy

The Policy Pivot: Inside India’s Strategic Shift

Edited by Ajay Khanna and Rahul Sharma

An edited volume on India’s shifting policy ecosystem. The contributors write about governance, corporate responsibility and public affairs, so it reads as policy more than foreign policy.

Partition South Asia

Shattered Lands: Five Partitions and the Making of Modern Asia

Sam Dalrymple

Dalrymple tells the whole story of how the Indian Empire was broken up, through five partitions and not only the one of 1947.

China Foreign Policy

Overreach: How China Derailed Its Peaceful Rise

Susan L. Shirk

Shirk, a former US official, argues that Xi Jinping’s more assertive foreign policy gave up China’s earlier cautious rise and ended up hurting China itself.

Indian Political Thought Biography

Hedgewar: A Definitive Biography

Sachin Nandha

A biography of K. B. Hedgewar, who founded the RSS in 1925, and the world he worked in. It is a way into the organisation’s origins.

Border Studies India-China

Through the India-China Border: Kalimpong in the Himalayas

Prem Poddar & Lisa Lindkvist Zhang (eds.)

Uses British, Chinese and Indian archives to look at Kalimpong as a border town. It shows how a place on the frontier helps explain the Tibet question in India-China relations.

Indian Political Thought South Asia

India, Bharat and Pakistan

J. Sai Deepak

The second book of the Bharat Trilogy, on the constitutional journey of what the author calls a sandwiched civilisation. I read it as a clear statement of the civilisational view of the Indian state.

Ancient India Archaeology

The Lost River: On the Trail of the Sarasvati

Michel Danino

Danino argues that the Sarasvati was a real river and asks what that would mean for early Indian history. The argument is still live among historians and archaeologists.

Military History Indian Air Force

The Sky Warriors: Operation Sindoor Unveiled

Vishnu Som

An inside account of Operation Sindoor from the pilots and planners who carried it out, with the air campaign at the centre.

Biography Indian Political Thought

Tilak: The Empire’s Biggest Enemy

Vaibhav Purandare

A biography of Bal Gangadhar Tilak, whom the British counted among their most dangerous opponents. It follows his politics and his idea of self-rule.

Rising Powers Indian Foreign Policy

Grasping Greatness: Making India a Leading Power

Ashley J. Tellis, Bibek Debroy & C. Raja Mohan

An edited volume on what it would take for India to become a leading power. It speaks directly to the question of how aspiration turns into standing.

World History Nehru

Glimpses of World History

Jawaharlal Nehru

Letters Nehru wrote to his daughter Indira from prison in the early 1930s, surveying the whole sweep of human history. They show how he read the world long before he had to run a country.

Diplomatic History Tibet

JFK’s Forgotten Crisis: Tibet, the CIA, and the Sino-Indian War

Bruce Riedel

Riedel rebuilds the American and CIA role around Tibet and the 1962 war from intelligence records. I read it next to Verma’s 1962 and Rao’s Fractured Himalaya.

India-China Diplomatic History

The Fractured Himalaya: India Tibet China 1949-1962

Nirupama Rao

A former Foreign Secretary’s account of the early years of India-China relations, from 1949 to 1962, using declassified material. It is central to my paper on recognition signalling.

Geopolitics Indian Foreign Policy

India and Asian Geopolitics: The Past, Present

Shivshankar Menon

A former National Security Adviser sets out how India should think about the shifting balance in Asia. It gives the practitioner’s view of questions I treat theoretically.
